The LLM Landscape: Architectures, Families, and How to Choose

The model you pick is rarely the whole story — the architecture underneath it, the license it ships under, and the family it belongs to shape what it can do and where you can run it. This is a field map of today's large language model ecosystem: the patterns that keep recurring, the vendors that define the frontier, and a practical lens for matching a family to a problem.

Why the Landscape Needs a Map

Two years ago, "which LLM should I use?" had a short answer. Today the question splits into a dozen sub-questions: open weights or API? Dense or mixture-of-experts? Reasoning-tuned or general-purpose? Long-context or compact? Hosted by a frontier lab or self-hosted on your own hardware?

The confusing part is that the names change faster than the structure. GPT, Claude, Gemini, Llama, Mistral, DeepSeek, Qwen, Phi — each ships new versions constantly, but they cluster into a surprisingly stable set of architectural patterns and strategic bets. Understanding the structure beneath the branding is what lets you make durable decisions.

The Core Idea

A model family is defined less by a single checkpoint and more by three durable commitments: its architecture (how it's built), its licensing posture (how you can use the weights), and its specialization (what it's tuned to be good at). Pick the family by those, not by last week's benchmark.

The First Fork: Open Weights vs. Closed APIs

The single most consequential split in the landscape is not quality — it's access. Frontier closed models are consumed through an API; open-weight models can be downloaded, inspected, fine-tuned, and run on your own infrastructure. This decision cascades into cost, privacy, customization, and operational burden.

Closed / API
Frontier Capability, Zero Infra
GPT, Claude, and Gemini lead on raw capability and are available instantly via API. You trade control and data residency for frontier quality and no GPU bills. Best when capability-per-call matters more than ownership.
Open Weights
Control, Privacy, Customization
Llama, Mistral, Gemma, Qwen, DeepSeek, Phi, and gpt-oss can be self-hosted and fine-tuned. You own the weights, keep data in-house, and avoid per-token pricing — at the cost of running and scaling the infrastructure yourself.
The Hybrid Reality
Most production stacks mix both: a frontier API for the hardest reasoning, a self-hosted open model for high-volume or privacy-sensitive paths. The line is a routing decision, not a one-time choice.
"Open" Is a Spectrum, Not a Switch

Open weights rarely mean open training data. Licenses range from permissive (Apache-2.0, as in many Mistral and Qwen releases) to community licenses with usage restrictions (some Llama releases). Always read the license before you build a business on a family.

Architecture Patterns Underneath the Hype

Almost every model in this post is a decoder-only Transformer. The interesting differentiation happens in a handful of design axes that recur across families.

Pattern 1
Dense Transformers
Every parameter fires on every token. Simple, predictable, easy to fine-tune and serve. The default for most Llama, Mistral, Gemma, and Phi models. Quality scales with size — but so does cost.
Pattern 2
Mixture of Experts (MoE)
Many expert sub-networks, but a router activates only a few per token. Huge total parameter counts with a fraction of the compute per token. Powers the efficiency of DeepSeek, Mixtral, and several frontier models.
Pattern 3
Reasoning / Test-Time Compute
Models trained to "think" in extended chains before answering, spending more compute at inference for harder problems. OpenAI's reasoning models and DeepSeek-R pioneered the open version of this pattern.
Pattern 4
Multimodal & Long-Context
Native handling of images, audio, and video alongside text, plus context windows stretching into the millions of tokens. Gemini and Qwen's multimodal lines lead here, reshaping what a single prompt can hold.
The Recurring Efficiency Moves

Beyond the headline patterns, families converge on the same efficiency tricks: grouped-query attention to shrink the KV cache, rotary position embeddings for longer context, and quantization (4-bit and below) to fit large models onto modest hardware. These are why a capable model can now run on a laptop.

Anatomy of a Modern LLM Stack

A deployed model is a layered system. Understanding the layers clarifies where a "family" actually lives — and where your engineering effort goes.

From Weights to Product
Application & Orchestration
Agents, RAG, tool use, guardrails — where product value is built
↑
Serving & Inference
APIs or runtimes (vLLM, Ollama, TGI), quantization, batching, KV cache
↑
Post-Training
Instruction tuning, RLHF/DPO, reasoning tuning, domain fine-tunes
↑
Base Model & Architecture
Pretrained Transformer weights — dense or MoE — the "family" core

A model family typically owns the bottom two layers (the base weights and the official post-trained variants). Everything above is yours. This is why two teams using the same Llama checkpoint can ship wildly different products — most of the differentiation lives in the top layers.

Eight vendors define most of today's practical landscape. Each has a distinct strategic bet — on openness, efficiency, reasoning, multimodality, or scale.

How to Choose: A Practical Lens

Instead of chasing the top of a leaderboard, work backward from constraints. Four questions usually settle the decision.

1 · Control
Do you need the weights?
If data residency, offline operation, or deep customization is required, start with open families — Llama, Mistral, Qwen, DeepSeek, Gemma, Phi, or gpt-oss. Otherwise a closed API is the fastest path to frontier quality.
2 · Task
What is the workload?
Hard reasoning and math favor reasoning-tuned lines (OpenAI reasoning, DeepSeek-R). Coding favors Claude, Codestral, Qwen, or DeepSeek. Multimodal and long-context favor Gemini and Qwen.
3 · Footprint
Where does it run?
On-device or tight budgets point to small models — Phi, small Gemma, or Mistral. Large-scale serving benefits from MoE efficiency. Match parameter count and quantization to the hardware you actually have.
4 · Economics
What does scale cost?
Per-token API pricing is simple until volume explodes; self-hosting flips the cost curve at scale but adds ops burden. DeepSeek and Mistral are frequent picks when cost-per-token dominates.
A Workable Default

Prototype against a strong closed API to validate the product, then route high-volume or privacy-sensitive paths to a self-hosted open family once the requirements are clear. Decide the model family last, after you understand the task, the footprint, and the economics — not first.

Takeaways

"The names change faster than the structure. Learn the patterns — dense vs. MoE, open vs. closed, general vs. reasoning — and the next release stops being a surprise."
— On navigating a fast-moving landscape

The LLM landscape looks chaotic because the surface churns weekly. Underneath, it's organized by a small number of durable choices: how the model is built, how you're allowed to use it, and what it's specialized for. Anchor on those and the ecosystem becomes navigable.

The Closing Thought

There is no single best model — only the best fit for a given task, footprint, and budget. The frontier labs set the ceiling on capability; the open families set the floor on access. The real skill is not picking a winner, but building a stack that routes each problem to the family that fits it.
