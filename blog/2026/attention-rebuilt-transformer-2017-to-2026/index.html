Attention, Rebuilt: The Transformer from 2017 to 2026

In June 2017, eight researchers at Google claimed recurrence — the backbone of sequence modeling for thirty years — was unnecessary. What replaced it was a single operation: let every token look at every other token, all at once. Nine years later the block diagram from that paper is still recognizable in every frontier model, even though nearly every square inside it has been swapped out. This is the story of what the block does, and why it kept changing.

The Anatomy of a Single Block

Strip away the branding and a Transformer is a stack of identical blocks operating on a residual stream. Let a sequence of \(n\) tokens be embedded into a matrix \(X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}\), where \(d\) is the model width. Each block does exactly two things: mix information across positions, then compute within each position.

Attention: The Mixing Operation

Every token projects into three vectors — a query, a key, and a value — through learned weight matrices. The attention output is then a softmax-weighted average of the values, where the weights are similarity scores between queries and keys:

\[ \text{Attn}(Q,K,V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}} + M\right)V \]

The \(\sqrt{d_k}\) divisor keeps dot products from growing with dimension, which would saturate the softmax and kill gradients. \(M\) is the mask: an upper-triangular matrix of \(-\infty\) for causal (decoder) attention, and zero for bidirectional (encoder) attention. That single mask is what separates a BERT from a GPT.

Multi-head attention splits the width into \(H\) parallel subspaces of size \(d_k = d/H\), runs attention independently in each, concatenates, and projects back through \(W_O\). The original paper used \(H = 8\) heads at \(d = 512\), so \(d_k = 64\). Heads are not redundant: interpretability work finds some track syntax, some track coreference, some attend only to the previous token.

Why Content-Addressable Routing Matters

Attention is a learned, content-addressable lookup: a token builds a query and retrieves a weighted blend of every other token's value. Unlike a fixed convolution kernel or a recurrent state, the routing itself is computed from the data. That is the property every later variant tries to preserve while paying less for it.

The Feed-Forward Network: The Computation

After mixing, each position is processed independently by a two-layer MLP applied identically across tokens — a linear projection up, a nonlinearity, and a linear projection back down. The hidden layer is conventionally \(4d\) wide, so the FFN holds roughly \(8d^2\) parameters against attention's \(4d^2\). That 2:1 ratio is why about two-thirds of a classical Transformer's parameters sit in the FFN — a fact that matters enormously once mixture-of-experts arrives, because the FFN is where sparsity gets applied.

Residuals and Normalization

Each sub-layer is wrapped as \(x \leftarrow x + \text{Sublayer}(\text{Norm}(x))\) in modern pre-norm form. The residual stream is a shared bus: every block reads from it and writes back additively, which is the mechanism that lets gradients reach layer 1 from layer 60 without decay.

The 2017 paper normalized after the sub-layer: \(x \leftarrow \text{Norm}(x + \text{Sublayer}(x))\). That works at six layers and becomes fragile at sixty, because every block rescales the stream the next block depends on. GPT-2's move to pre-norm — normalize the input, leave the residual untouched — was arguably a bigger enabler of depth than any other single change between 2018 and 2022.

Positional Encoding

Attention is permutation-invariant: shuffle the tokens and the softmax output shuffles with them. Positions must therefore be injected. The original used fixed sinusoids of geometrically spaced frequencies added to the embeddings; later models learned absolute position tables; both were later replaced by rotary embeddings, covered below.

The Three Topologies

Topology Mask Objective Canonical model Best at
Encoder-only None (bidirectional) Masked token prediction BERT Classification, retrieval, embeddings
Decoder-only Causal Next-token prediction GPT, LLaMA Open-ended generation, in-context learning
Encoder–decoder Bidirectional + causal, plus cross-attention Sequence-to-sequence T5, BART Translation, summarization, structured transforms

The original paper was an encoder–decoder built for WMT14 English–German translation, at 65M parameters for the base model and 213M for the large one, with six layers in each stack. Both stacks are architecturally identical apart from the mask; the decoder inserts a third sub-layer that cross-attends into the encoder's output, where \(Q\) comes from the decoder and \(K, V\) from the encoder.

The Cost Model

The crossover is around \(n \approx d\). Below it, the FFN dominates and the Transformer is essentially free; above it, attention's quadratic term takes over — and every architectural innovation from 2023 onward is aimed at that term.

Why It Displaced Recurrence — and the Bill That Came Due

An RNN compresses history into a hidden state it updates one step at a time. Information from token 1 must traverse \(n\) sequential operations to influence token \(n\), and gradients must traverse them backwards. Attention collapses that path to a single step: any token reaches any other through one dot product. Training also parallelizes across the entire sequence, which is what made GPUs the right substrate and thousand-GPU runs feasible.

What the Transformer gave up was inductive bias. Convolution bakes in locality; recurrence bakes in temporal order. Attention bakes in nothing — so it needs more data to learn that nearby words matter, and, once given data at scale, it learns better structure than any hand-designed prior. That trade is the entire scaling story.

The bill: the KV cache

To generate token-by-token, a decoder must store every past key and value, because they are reused for each new query. The memory cost is:

\[ \text{bytes} = 2 \times L \times H_{kv} \times d_{head} \times n_{ctx} \times \text{batch} \times \text{precision} \]

For LLaMA-3-70B, the per-token KV dimension is \(2 \times 8 \times 128 = 2048\) across 80 layers, or roughly 320 KiB per token in bf16. At an 8K context that is about 2.6 GB per sequence — before weights, activations, or batching. Under classic multi-head attention with 64 KV heads it would be eight times worse. This single number explains more architecture decisions since 2023 than any accuracy benchmark.

Two Consequences

Specialized kernels: FlashAttention reformulated the softmax to tile through fast on-chip SRAM instead of materializing the full \(n \times n\) score matrix in HBM — making exact attention dramatically faster without changing a line of the math. Cache compression then became a first-class design problem, producing MQA, GQA, and MLA.

2018–2022: Three Lineages, One Lesson

The original architecture was treated as a menu, and the field split three ways within a year. GPT-1 (117M) kept only the decoder and showed that generative pre-training on unlabeled text transfers; BERT kept only the encoder and showed that masked prediction produces powerful representations; T5 later reframed every task as text-to-text inside an encoder–decoder.

Alongside the topology split, a quiet sequence of component fixes accumulated. Pre-norm replaced post-norm after GPT-2. Learned absolute embeddings gave way to relative position biases in T5. PaLM (540B) introduced SwiGLU activations and parallelized the attention and FFN branches. ViT showed that an image is just a sequence of patches, and the same block learned vision — the moment "Transformer" stopped being an NLP term.

The Scaling Laws That Reframed Everything

Kaplan et al. (2020) showed loss falls as a smooth power law in parameters, data, and compute — making architecture search partly subordinate to simply building bigger. Chinchilla (2022) corrected the recipe: for a fixed compute budget, most models were badly undertrained, and roughly 20 tokens per parameter was closer to optimal. That correction is why LLaMA trained a 7B model on a trillion tokens instead of a 70B model on 300B.

By 2022 the block had stabilized in outline — pre-norm, decoder-only, learned everything — but every internal component was still the 2017 default.

The Modern Block, Term by Term

LLaMA (February 2023) marked the moment the field converged on a canonical recipe: decoder-only, pre-norm RMSNorm, SwiGLU, rotary position embeddings, no biases in linear layers. Almost every open model since is a variation on this template, with innovation concentrated in the attention layer and the FFN.

Normalization: RMSNorm

RMSNorm drops mean-centering and the bias term, dividing each activation vector by its own root-mean-square and scaling by a learned gain. Fewer operations, no re-centering, empirically equal stability. Every model in the modern lineage uses it.

Activation: SwiGLU

The FFN becomes a gated linear unit: two parallel projections, one passed through a Swish nonlinearity, multiplied elementwise, then projected down. It costs a third matrix, so hidden widths are usually scaled to \(\tfrac{8}{3}d\) to keep parameter counts comparable. The gain is a consistent loss improvement at matched compute.

Positions: RoPE

Rotary position embedding removes positional vectors entirely. Instead, each query and key is rotated by an angle proportional to its absolute position, applied in 2-D pairs across the head dimension with geometrically spaced frequencies (a base of 10,000, raised to 500,000 in long-context models). The trick is that rotations compose, so the dot product of a rotated query and a rotated key depends only on the relative offset between them. Relative position falls out of the geometry, with zero learned parameters. It also explains context extension: raising the base frequency slows the rotation so distant positions stay distinguishable, which is what YaRN and similar methods exploit.

Grouped-query attention is the pragmatic middle: query heads are partitioned into groups that share a key/value head, so quality stays close to full MHA while the cache shrinks by the group ratio. It is the production default across the 2025–2026 model generation.

Multi-head latent attention is the more radical move. Instead of caching per-head keys and values, MLA down-projects the hidden state into one shared latent vector \(c^{KV}_t\) of width 512, caches only that, and up-projects back to per-head keys and values at attention time. Two details make it work. First, the up-projection matrices fold into the query projection during decoding ("query absorption"), so no decompression happens at runtime. Second, RoPE would break that folding, so a small separate 64-dimensional rotary key is cached alongside — decoupled RoPE.

The arithmetic is stark. DeepSeek-V3 caches \(512 + 64 = 576\) elements per token per layer, versus LLaMA-3-70B's 2048 — and with 61 layers versus 80, the total lands near 70 KB per token, roughly a seventh of the dense equivalent, with quality that matches or exceeds it.

Feed-Forward: Dense to Sparse Mixture-of-Experts

The FFN is where MoE lives, because it holds most parameters. An MoE block replaces the single MLP with \(N\) expert MLPs plus a small router that scores each token and sends it to only its top few experts.

Each token activates only \(k\) of \(N\) experts — typically 2 to 4 of 64 to 256. Total parameters can then scale into the hundreds of billions while per-token FLOPs stay near a much smaller dense model. Two engineering problems dominate: load balancing (a router that collapses onto a few experts wastes capacity, hence auxiliary balancing losses or bias-based adjustment) and communication (experts scatter across devices, so all-to-all traffic replaces all-reduce).

Long Context: Interleaved Attention

The cheapest way to tame quadratic cost is to stop paying it every layer. Modern stacks alternate local and global attention: most layers attend only within a fixed window (a few hundred to a few thousand tokens), while every few layers attend globally to stitch long-range information together.

Gemma 2 and 3 alternate local and global layers; Qwen3 exposes a per-layer attention_type field so a config can mix full and sliding attention arbitrarily; gpt-oss goes further, alternating 128-token sliding windows with full attention at a 1:1 ratio across its layers. This preserves the one property that matters — the network can still move information across arbitrary distances — while cutting the average cost per layer.

Stability: The Unglamorous Fixes

Three small changes now appear almost universally:

Fix 1
QK-norm
Applies RMSNorm to queries and keys per head before the dot product, capping logit magnitude. Qwen3 adopted it explicitly for training stability, and also removed QKV biases.
Fix 2
Attention sinks
Add a learned scalar to the softmax denominator so a head can attend to nothing. Without it, every head must place probability mass somewhere, and early tokens get hijacked as dumping grounds. gpt-oss learns one sink bias per head.
Fix 3
Dropout removed
With single-epoch training on trillions of tokens, each example is seen once — so regularization by dropout hurts more than it helps.

Where Transformers Are Actively Used

The block that started as a machine-translation model now underpins most of modern AI. The same residual-stream-plus-attention recipe generalizes to anything that can be serialized into a sequence of tokens — text, pixels, audio frames, amino acids, or robot actions. A few domains dominate real-world deployment.

Language
Chat, Search, Coding, Agents
The flagship use case: assistants (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini), coding tools (Copilot, Cursor), enterprise RAG over internal docs, summarization, translation, and the agentic systems built on top of all of it. Decoder-only models dominate here.
Retrieval & Embeddings
Semantic Search & Ranking
Encoder-style models turn text into dense vectors that power semantic search, recommendation, deduplication, classification, and the retrieval half of every RAG pipeline. Quiet but enormous — this runs under most production search today.
Vision & Multimodal
Images, Video, Documents
Vision Transformers (ViT) treat an image as a sequence of patches, powering classification, detection, segmentation, OCR, and document understanding. Multimodal models (GPT-4o, Gemini, Qwen-VL) fuse text and pixels for captioning, visual Q&A, and UI agents.
Generative Media
Image, Audio & Video Synthesis
Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) back image and video generators (Stable Diffusion 3, Sora-style models). Text-to-speech and music generation (speech synthesis, audio codecs) are increasingly Transformer-based sequence models over audio tokens.
Science & Biology
Proteins, Genomics, Chemistry
Attention over amino-acid sequences drives protein structure and function prediction (AlphaFold's Evoformer, ESM); related models handle DNA/RNA, molecular property prediction, and drug discovery. Sequences of biological "tokens" fit the recipe naturally.
Time Series & Tabular
Forecasting & Anomaly Detection
Transformer forecasters model demand, energy load, sensor telemetry, and financial series, capturing long-range seasonality that classical models miss. Foundation models for time series (e.g. TimesFM-style) bring zero-shot forecasting to new signals.
Robotics & Control
Policies & World Models
Decision/trajectory Transformers and vision-language-action models (RT-2, OpenVLA) map observations to actions, letting a single policy generalize across tasks. The sequence here is a stream of states, actions, and goals.
Telecom & Networks
Signals, Logs & Operations
In mobile networks, sequence models support channel estimation, beam prediction, traffic forecasting, log anomaly detection, and AIOps over telemetry — domains where ordered, high-volume data maps cleanly onto attention.
The Unifying Pattern

What makes the Transformer a general substrate is that it imposes almost no assumptions about the data. If a problem can be expressed as a sequence of tokens and the right relationships are long-range and content-dependent, the same block tends to work — which is why one architecture now spans language, vision, audio, biology, and control.

Beyond Attention: State-Space Models and Hybrids

The most serious challenge to attention came from Albert Gu and Tri Dao's Mamba (late 2023). A selective state-space model keeps a fixed-size recurrent state and updates it one step at a time, but with input-dependent transition matrices — so the model can choose what to remember and what to forget based on the token it just saw. Because the state has fixed size, training is a parallel scan and decoding costs constant memory per token — \(O(n)\) rather than \(O(n^2)\), with no KV cache at all.

Mamba-2 (2024) then showed that structured state-space models and linear attention are mathematically dual, which reframed the debate: these are not rivals to attention, they are attention with a compressed memory.

And that is exactly how production settled it. Pure SSMs and pure RWKV models lose on short-context tasks and on exact recall, where attention's direct token-to-token lookup is hard to beat. So the field built hybrids: mostly cheap state-space layers, punctuated by full-attention layers every sixth to eighth block to restore precise retrieval. Jamba mixes Mamba, attention, and MoE; IBM's Granite 4 is a Mamba-2 hybrid reporting roughly 70% lower inference cost; Falcon-H1 and Nemotron-H follow the same pattern.

What Never Changed

Five invariants survive all nine years:

Residual stream. Every sub-layer reads and writes it additively.
Pre-sublayer normalization. Normalize the input, not the sum.
Mix then compute. Cross-token mixing alternates with per-token computation.
Depth as the lever. Capability scales primarily with depth, compounded by scale.
Simple objective. Next-token or masked prediction, which architecture rarely touches.

The Thesis Worth Arguing

"Every generation re-litigates one trade: attention buys exact, global, content-addressable routing between any two tokens — and charges quadratic time plus a cache that grows with context."
— The through-line of nine years

Each era's hardware decides where that line lands. In 2017 the constraint was GPU parallelism, so recurrence died. In 2023 the constraint was inference memory bandwidth — nearly every architecture paper since is a bandwidth paper. In 2026 the constraint is context length, which is why the winning answer is not "replace attention" but "use less of it, where it isn't needed."

The Closing Thought

The 2017 block diagram is still on the page. What changed is every square inside it — norm moved before the sub-layer, position moved into the rotation, the FFN sprouted experts, attention learned to cache a latent instead of every head. The outline survived precisely because it was never the point. The point was a residual stream that alternates mixing and computation, and a hardware budget that keeps deciding how much mixing you can afford.
